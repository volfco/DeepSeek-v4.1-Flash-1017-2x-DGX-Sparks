### Snapshot availability changes the held-prefix decision

Keep the existing 10 s held-prefix policy for general serving. In a controlled 3:1 placement with four 384-token budgets, the 500 ms challenger reduced the longest queue from 13.620 to 6.228 s. It also evicted an unbacked session whose next visit took 152.47 s to first token and reused zero tokens. The control kept 296,960 tokens and served that revisit in 1.04 s. The other returning histories retained 299,008 tokens in each arm and all burst inputs and outputs matched. The experimental guard checks for a committed copy on both ranks. At the production 2,048-token persistence floor, the burst and three returns took 24.671 s with the guard, 29.163 s with ten-second patience and 179.827 s with unconditional 500 ms. In the independent reverse-order 1,024-token burst, the guard and plain 500 ms had the same 5.98–5.99 s maximum queue. The guard retained 296,960 tokens and served the return in 1.38 s versus 151.29 s for the plain policy. In the fully backed 384-token case, the guard took 30.548 s across the sequence versus 24.658 s for plain 500 ms. Each case has one paired observation. Those fully backed arms differed in whether their final preparatory fill was cold or restored. In the matched all-warm reverse-order repeat, the guarded sequence took 29.356 s and the plain sequence took 30.144 s. With a naturally stopping 142-token lead, short-held fixed grace finished in 3.484 s and short-held adaptive grace in 10.815 s. Retain fixed finish grace. Keep the guard experimental. In a real two-rank CPU loopback, a stalled peer blocked the proposing thread for 10.010 s and broke the spill agreement, invoking its registered break callback. The serving build connects this callback to admission shutdown and bounded drain. The frequency of peer stalls and of missing-copy revisits matters to general serving.
